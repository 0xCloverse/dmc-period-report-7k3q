What happened

Our reading of the period. The numbers behind each point are further down.

  1. No decision was changed from the defaults in Practice Round 1. Every price, discount, ad budget, theme, promotion, production level, dealer count, dividend and debt level is identical to Start.
  2. The +$734M net income jump is almost entirely an accounting roll-off, not better business. Start carried a one-off $1,223M R&D charge (all five firms built their second development center then). Year 1 had no R&D. Gross margin rose only $25M on $765M more sales.
  3. Margins are eroding. Gross margin fell from 25.5% to 24.7% because COGS grew 5.0% while sales grew 3.8%. Materials and labor costs rose and prices were not raised to match. This will compound every period if MSRPs stay flat.
  4. Interest expense rose $94M ($708M to $803M) with no change in debt. Carrying $9,444M of short-term debt is getting more expensive.
  5. Firm D finished Year 1 first in net income ($2,035M) and stock price ($57.80), but every firm got the same R&D roll-off boost. Firm D got the largest boost because it had the largest Start R&D charge. The ranking flatters the underlying business.
  6. Delite lost leadership of microsegment 1E to Alec this period. At Start, Delite led 1E; in Year 1, Alec leads.
  7. Competitor shortages are confirmed in the export: Boffo (Firm B) 'demand>production' and Cafav (Firm C) 'shortage 10-30%'. Firms B and C run at 3.7 and 4.4 days of inventory. Unmet demand is sitting in the Family and Luxury classes.
  8. Firm B is the only firm investing in R&D ($772M) and raised its Styling and Quality ceilings from 8 to 9. B already has the highest firm preference (26.6%). The premium gap is widening.
  9. Our own inventory is thin: Detonka 13 days, Defy 16 days, against a 30-day norm. Without a production increase the next period risks our own shortage.
  10. The Economic Outlook forecasts dealer truck sales rising from 1,425k to 1,612k (+13%) in Year 2. Detonka is the only Firm D truck.
  11. Correction to earlier analysis: all five firms spend almost the same on marketing ($377M-$436M). Firm D is not under-spending relative to peers. Firm A, not Firm D, carries the most debt ($10,539M vs $9,444M).

What happened

Our reading of the period. The numbers behind each point are further down.

  1. The team changed nearly every marketing lever this period, unlike Year 1: MSRP, dealer discount, advertising, theme, promotion and production on all three cars, plus corporate advertising ($107M to $135M) and dealer training ($25M to $30M).
  2. Delite now loses money on every car sold. MSRP was cut from $11,293 to $10,999 and the dealer discount raised from 9% to 20%, so revenue per unit fell to $8,801 against a variable cost of $9,069. Contribution swung from +$281M to -$171M. This is the low-margin price-cut trap: at a 12.5% margin there was no room to cut price.
  3. Production was allocated the wrong way round. Delite, the loss-maker, was raised from 268k to 331k and ended at 125 days of inventory. Defy and Detonka, the profitable cars, ended at 3 days each and were close to stocking out.
  4. Paying down $3,000M of short-term debt was a good move: interest expense fell $287M, about half of the gross-margin loss. Short-term debt is now $6,444M at 6.5%, and the bond rating is BBB, up from B at the start.
  5. Gross margin fell $562M and gross margin % fell from 24.7% to 21.6%. Net income fell $342M to $1,693M. Cumulative net income is $5,028M.
  6. Firm D is now the only firm with zero R&D. Firm A raised all four technology ceilings (Safety now 5, equal to ours), Firm C raised three, Firm E built a third development center, and A/C/E each spent $1.0B-$1.4B on R&D.
  7. The development projects entered for this period (Delite major upgrade, Dune, third development center) did not run. R&D is $0 in the Year 2 income statement.
  8. Firm E upgraded Euro across the board (size, HP, and all four ISSQ attributes) and Euro now leads microsegment 3U as well as 5U. This is the Utility space the Dune concept was aimed at.
  9. Competitors moved their ad themes toward hot buttons. Estruck (Detonka's rival in trucks) switched to a Quality theme and doubled advertising to $120M. Quality is a 1T hot button.
  10. Demand is strong across the market. All five firms ran over capacity (A 116%, B 103%, C 117%, D 104%, E 129%). Microsegments 2F, 1T and 3T grew about 10%, and the truck sales forecast rises another 13% next period.
  11. The team ordered 300k units of new capacity, which comes on line next period. Running at 104% this period cost a $95M over-capacity charge. Firms A and B each ordered 100k.
  12. Firm D's firm preference fell again, from 15.2% to 12.7%, still last of five. Firm E now leads on stock price, unit share, value share and preference.
